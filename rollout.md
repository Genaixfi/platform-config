# Rollout Strategy

## Objective

Deploy Copilot Enterprise and AI-augmented engineering workflows across Genaixfi in waves that optimize adoption and reduce risk.

## Rollout model

### Wave 0 — Foundation

- confirm org owner and platform ownership
- assign initial seats
- set repo protections and access defaults
- establish governance and review workflows

### Wave 1 — Pilot teams

- onboard 1-2 small engineering teams
- focus on repo-aware understanding, PR review, and debugging
- collect qualitative feedback and operational issues

### Wave 2 — Expansion

- add more engineering groups
- standardize prompts and repo-specific guidance
- document successful patterns and common pitfalls

### Wave 3 — Org-wide maturity

- expand to broader engineering and platform workflows
- operationalize metrics and governance reviews
- tune policy and feature access based on evidence

## Non-linear execution lens

Genaixfi's rollout should not be treated as a simple linear sequence. In practice, capability adoption moves through feedback loops:

- discovery triggers configuration changes
- configuration changes create new integration patterns
- integration patterns reveal security or policy needs
- policy tuning unlocks additional adoption waves

This means the process is iterative, recursive, and adaptive rather than strictly stepwise.

## Success metrics

- seat activation rate
- active team usage by week
- time saved in common engineering tasks
- reduction in onboarding cycles
- review quality and defect detection improvement
- security incidents or policy exceptions

## Recommendation

The most effective path is to treat Copilot adoption as a platform transformation project with technical, social, and governance dimensions. Build it in small calibrated waves, not as a single org-wide switch.
